# Reader and navigation

Verse URLs are `/{book}/{chapter}/{verse}`. [navigation.ts](../../src/lib/navigation.ts) validates locations and computes adjacent verses across chapter/book boundaries, stopping at the ends of the Bible. The root and `/verse/[number]` routes provide redirects.

## The spread

A verse page is the **spread** ([spread.tsx](../../src/components/spread/spread.tsx)), the reader's one screen for one verse, built for the redesign in [#434](https://github.com/AustinKelsay/visibible/issues/434). It is dark whatever the system setting is, unless the reader chooses light or following the system in Advanced (light: a light glass words page over the same picture, with the dock, layers and credits dialog in matching light surfaces; the whole-picture view and the unpainted picture area stay dark) ([Theme](THEME.md)).

- **Computer (768 px and wider).** The [picture](../../src/components/spread/spread-picture.tsx) covers the window, cropped from its centre. The [words page](../../src/components/spread/words-page.tsx) lies over the right side as dark glass: a line naming the book and chapter, the verse number large, the verse in a serif face, and a foot line with the translation name and the position ("5 / 51"). A floating dock on the picture holds previous, the passage name (it opens Contents), next and "Aa Look" (it opens Look).
- **Verse marks** ([verse-marks.tsx](../../src/components/spread/verse-marks.tsx)): a thin row along the top of the picture, one mark per verse of the chapter. A painted verse, one the picture rule finds a picture for, is brighter (`getChapterContents` with the same verse ids and style order Contents uses, so opening Contents finds its wall already loaded), the current verse is white, thicker and `aria-current="page"`, and each mark is a link named "Verse 7, painted" or "Verse 8, unpainted". Marks are not prefetched (the whole chapter is on screen).
- **Phone.** The picture sits above in a square frame (at most about half the screen) and the words page fills the rest. Previous and next sit at the ends of the foot line, with the translation name and the position together in its middle; a dock under the verse holds the Contents button, "Ask a question…" and "Aa" (Look).
- **The picture rule** ([picture-rule.ts](../../src/lib/picture-rule.ts)) decides which picture a verse shows: (1) the newest saved picture in the chosen art style; (2) otherwise the newest in another style the reader may see, taking styles in the work's style-set order (the Bible brief's `styleIds`); (3) otherwise the verse is unpainted. The styles a reader may see are the public styles, plus owner-only styles (Byzantine Icon) for an admin session (`useVisibleArtStyles`). `usePictureStyles()` in [work-scope-context.tsx](../../src/context/work-scope-context.tsx) gives the order (chosen style first, then the other visible styles), and every read that chooses a picture passes it: the spread (`getSpreadPicture`), the next spread's prefetch, the verse marks and Contents (`getChapterContents`), and the unpainted glow (`getNearestEarlierPicture`). The database half is `newestPictureInStyles` in [verseImages.ts](../../convex/verseImages.ts); `readerStyles` there drops owner-only styles for anyone but an admin, whatever the client asks for. A saved choice the reader may not see (an owner-only style for a reader who is no longer admin) reads as the first visible style without being overwritten.
- **The picture** is the one the rule chooses (`getSpreadPicture`), unless a version was chosen in the picture menu. Its thumbnail shows first and the full picture replaces it once loaded. While it is read the frame is plain dark. Loading the full picture records one impression for it.
- **A verse painted only in another style** shows that picture with a note on it (top left under the corner name on a computer, above the picture menu's chip on a phone): "Shown in Ink & Watercolor. No Classical picture of this verse yet." and **Paint in Classical · about N credits**, priced and charged exactly like the unpainted verse's paint button ([paint-verse.tsx](../../src/components/spread/paint-verse.tsx); "Get credits to paint" when the balance cannot cover it, no price for an admin). Painting shows progress in the note; the new picture replaces the shown one once saved, and the note goes with it. The automatic-painting switch also paints such a verse, since it has no picture in the chosen style. The offer fades with the other controls while idle; progress, a failure or a price to confirm does not.
- **Whole-picture view** ([spread-picture.tsx](../../src/components/spread/spread-picture.tsx), [whole-picture.tsx](../../src/components/spread/whole-picture.tsx)): the picture is a button named "Show the whole picture of John 1:5". Activating it (pointer, Enter or Space) opens the `whole-picture` layer: the words page slides off to the right on a computer (hidden on a phone, `inert` on both), and the picture is shown uncropped (`object-fit: contain`) on dark, whatever its shape, with the verse under its reference as a caption. On a computer the caption lies over the foot of the picture above the dock, and lets taps through to the picture; the dock and the marks span the whole width. On a phone the picture and caption sit centred above a row with previous, the position and next (the phone dock with Contents and Ask is put aside with the words page). Activating the picture again (now named "Back to the words") or Escape returns to the spread and puts focus on the picture. Previous and next (dock, phone row, arrow keys, swipe, marks) turn the page and the next spread opens in the view too (`useArrivesInWholePicture` in page-turn.tsx, carried with the last turn like the arrival slide); a spread reached any other way opens with its words, and a turned-to verse with no picture gives the words back. Opening it reports `whole_picture_opened`. A verse with no picture offers no view. The dock and the marks fade in this view as on the spread; the caption does not.
- **Moving pictures** (#447, #480): a picture with its own finished video (`verseVideos.getPictureVideo` with the shown picture's id: a version chosen in the picture menu, else the picture rule's) shows Play ([play-mark.tsx](../../src/components/spread/play-mark.tsx)), a glass "Play" button named "Play the video of John 1:5" at the bottom-right of the visible picture (of the window in the whole-picture view on a computer; on the picture's corner in that view on a phone, in one row that takes no room, so pressing Play moves nothing). A video of another version or another style of the verse shows no Play until that picture is shown, so choosing a version in the picture menu or a style in Look moves or hides it. An unpainted verse never shows Play, nor does a picture whose only videos are still being made or failed. Play does not fade with the other controls, so a reader passing by sees that the picture moves; nothing plays until it is pressed. Pressing it makes the picture move where it is ([spread-picture.tsx](../../src/components/spread/spread-picture.tsx)): the video (muted at first, without browser controls, hidden from screen readers since the picture keeps its description) lies over the picture inside the picture's own button, at the picture's scale and centred like it in whichever fit the frame uses (the clip is the picture's centre a little narrower at the same height), and fades in once it has a frame, so the painting comes alive without a jump. Where the frame is wider than the clip (a very wide window, or the whole-picture view) the clip's side edges fade into the picture, so no hard seam runs between moving and still. The words page, the layout and focus stay; on a computer's spread the video stops at the words page's edge so the glass is not redrawn every frame (the page's width, `min(40vw, 600px)`, comes from [words-page-width.ts](../../src/components/spread/words-page-width.ts) for both the CSS variable and this clip). Play becomes Pause ("Pause the video of John 1:5"), then Play to resume, and when the clip ends it holds its last frame and the button reads Play again. While it waits for its first frame the mark shows a ring after a short wait; a quick second press does nothing, and once the ring shows a press cancels; a browser that refuses to start it leaves the picture and Play. Beside it a sound button ("Sound of the video of John 1:5", pressed when on) turns the clip's sound on; the choice holds for the visit, and a browser that refuses to start a clip with sound (as for a clip Continuous play starts) plays that clip muted, keeping the choice. Choosing another version or style while it moves ends it, and coming back starts from Play. Escape, when no layer is open, stops it and brings the picture back with focus on Play. Opening the whole-picture view while it moves keeps the same video playing, uncropped. **Continuous play** is offered above it (beside it in the phone's whole-picture view) while a video is pressed and the next verse's picture (by the picture rule) has a finished video too, except to a reader who asked for reduced motion. Turned on, it fetches that clip ahead; when the clip ends it turns the page (`verse_navigation` with source `play_on`) and the next spread arrives already playing its picture's video with Continuous play still on (`useArrivesPlaying` in page-turn.tsx, carried with the last turn like the whole-picture view), announcing "Now playing John 1:6" to screen readers. A reader busy with a layer (Contents, Look, a menu) or typing when a clip ends is not carried away: the clip holds its last frame and the run ends. The run also ends at a verse whose picture has no video; a turn by hand or a verse chosen in Contents (which Continuous play never overrides: `useTurnedAway` and `noteTurnAway` in page-turn.tsx; a click that opens a verse in another tab is not a turn, `opensElsewhere`), Escape or turning Continuous play off ends it too, and a spread reached any other way opens on its still picture. Continuous play and the sound button stand above Play (beside it, in one row, in the phone's whole-picture view); below a wide window (1280 px) the controls sit above the spread's dock row, so they never cover it, and in the whole-picture view they stay low beside the centred dock, clear of the caption. On a phone the picture menu's chip shortens a long label so it never meets Play (only when Play is there), the other-style note stays mounted (hidden in the whole-picture view), and while a video is pressed its idle offer hides (a paint it started carries on, its progress, failure or price to confirm shown above the controls).
- **The picture menu** ([picture-menu.tsx](../../src/components/spread/picture-menu.tsx)) is a glass chip at the picture's bottom-left whenever the spread shows a picture (also one from another style), reading the shown picture's style and its place among the chosen style's versions ("Classical · 2 of 3"; only the style when it is the sole picture or from another style). It joins the idle fade and sits beside the picture's button, not in it, so its taps never open the whole-picture view; it hides in that view. It opens the `picture` layer (a bottom sheet on a phone, a panel over the chip on a computer): the verse's pictures as thumbnails ([picture-source.ts](../../src/lib/picture-source.ts)), the chosen style newest first, then the other styles the reader may see in the style set's order (`getImageHistory` with `otherStyles`, read only while open; the visible styles from `useVisibleArtStyles`). The shown one is ringed and focused. Choosing one shows it on the spread ([chosen-picture.tsx](../../src/components/spread/chosen-picture.tsx)) until the page turns; coming back shows the newest. A thumbnail whose picture has a finished video (`verseVideos.getPicturesWithVideo` with the listed pictures' ids, read while the menu is mounted, so no cap on the verse's videos hides a badge) carries a small play badge and its name ends ", has a video"; choosing it brings its Play onto the spread. Under the list: **Paint another** ([Image generation](IMAGE-GENERATION.md#painting-on-the-spread)), **Make this picture move…** when video generation is on, with its inline review ([Video generation](VIDEO-GENERATION.md#making-a-video-from-the-picture-menu)), **Details** (style, date, size and aspect ratio, model, cost in credits) and **Report this picture**, which replaces the menu with the feedback form about the shown picture ([Feedback](FEEDBACK.md)). Escape, a tap outside or, on a phone, its close button ("Close picture menu") closes it and focus returns to the chip. Opening it reports `picture_menu_opened`.
- **An unpainted verse** (no picture in any style the reader may see) shows [the unpainted picture area](../../src/components/spread/unpainted-picture.tsx): a blurred glow of the nearest earlier picture of the chapter by the same rule (`getNearestEarlierPicture`, its thumbnail when it has one), or plain dark when no earlier verse has one, then "No picture for John 1:6 yet", one paint button and the switch "Paint verses automatically as I read". [Image generation](IMAGE-GENERATION.md#painting-on-the-spread) describes the button, painting and the switch.
- **A long verse** scrolls inside the words page; the picture and the foot line stay put.
- **Turning pages** ([page-turn.tsx](../../src/components/spread/page-turn.tsx)): previous and next move one verse across chapter and book boundaries ([navigation.ts](../../src/lib/navigation.ts)). The left and right arrow keys do the same unless the reader is typing in a field ([keyboard helper](../../src/lib/verse-keyboard-navigation.ts)). Each turn reports `verse_navigation` with source `keyboard`, `dock`, `swipe` or `mark` (`contents` for a Contents tile).
- **Swipe** ([swipe-turn.ts](../../src/components/spread/swipe-turn.ts)): a one-finger horizontal swipe anywhere on the spread turns the page (leftward = next). The first 10 px of movement decide: a touch that starts up or down is a scroll and never turns, so a long verse still scrolls; a turn also needs 56 px sideways and more sideways than vertical travel. Two fingers, a touch that starts in a text field, or an open layer other than the whole-picture view turn nothing.
- **The next spread is fetched ahead** ([prefetch-next.tsx](../../src/components/spread/prefetch-next.tsx)): `router.prefetch` of the next address, a subscription to the next verse's `getSpreadPicture` with the same arguments the picture reads (so the next spread finds it already loaded), and `preload` of its thumbnail and full picture.
- **Arriving.** A turned page's words slide in from the side it was turned towards and its picture fades up (`data-arrival` on the spread, `data-turn-in` on the animated parts, CSS in globals.css). Arriving any other way (a link, reload, back button) does not animate.
- **Fading controls** ([idle-fade.ts](../../src/components/spread/idle-fade.ts)): on a computer the dock, the marks and the corner name fade after 3 seconds without pointer, wheel, touch, focus or keyboard activity and come back on any. Nothing fades while a layer other than the whole-picture view is open or while focus is inside one of the fading controls. The spread carries `data-idle`; any element marked `data-fades` inside it fades (CSS in globals.css, 768 px and wider only, so nothing fades on a phone). Faded controls stay in the accessibility tree and keep their pointer events.
- **The map** ([#532](https://github.com/AustinKelsay/visibible/issues/532); [place-map.tsx](../../src/components/spread/place-map.tsx), [place-map-view.tsx](../../src/components/spread/place-map-view.tsx)). A Bible verse with a **setting** has one link under the verse on the words page: "See where this happened", in the Ask suggestions' serif italic style, on a phone and a computer.
  - **The setting** comes from [verse-setting.ts](../../src/lib/verse-setting.ts): the places the verse names, otherwise those of the nearest earlier verse of its chapter that names a place, otherwise none. The verse page resolves it on the server and passes it to the spread, so the browser needs no places data until the map opens. Psalm 23 and the first verses of John 1 have no setting and no link. A carried region can be loose: Exodus 14:20 names Egypt for the Egyptians' camp, so 14:21 is set in Egypt.
  - **The map takes the picture's place**: left of the words page on a computer, the square frame on a phone. The picture is hidden under it with its controls (still mounted, so painting already under way carries on); a playing video stops and Continuous play ends. Automatic painting waits while the map is open ([#561](https://github.com/AustinKelsay/visibible/issues/561)): turning pages with it open paints nothing behind it, and closing it paints the verse then in view once. The verse marks, corner name and dock stay above it. It is drawn from the [map outline](../../src/data/bible-map-outline.json) and framed on the setting's sites, at least 3.5° across (9° for a setting of only regions), in the band between the close button and the note, so neither covers a place. With Ask open on a phone, the note keeps only its credit.
    - A settled place is a pin with its name.
    - A debated place (more than one proposed site) is a ring at each site, sized by the site's score. A lone debated place names each site; several name their most favoured site.
    - A settled region (Egypt) is a wide label.
    - Places at the same spot share one marker and one name ("Jerusalem · Zion"; Baal-zephon and Pi-hahiroth both proposed at Al Mahamdiyah).
    - Names are placed after every marker, each with a dark edge: right of the marker, else left, above, below or at a corner, never over another name, a marker's centre or a region's name, and inside the band. A name with no room is left out; its card still names it. A region's name moves up to three lines up or down, or to either side, to clear the rings, inside the band. A name answers a tap or the pointer as its marker does.
    - Pins and rings are buttons named for what they show ("Migdol: proposed at Tell el Herr, the most favoured of 3 proposed sites"; equal scores are "tied as the most favoured"). Rings are drawn largest first, so a smaller ring is never hidden under another.
    - Well-known places around the setting are named faintly with small dots, not interactive. They are named places with a clearly favoured site and at least five verses, leaving out regions and names said with "the" (rivers, seas, valleys). The most named come first, at most ten, only where a name fits clear of the setting's markers, names and regions.
  - **The place card** ([#556](https://github.com/AustinKelsay/visibible/issues/556); [place-card.tsx](../../src/components/spread/place-card.tsx), chapters from [place-chapters.ts](../../src/lib/place-chapters.ts)).
    - Hovering or focusing a marker previews its card, which is hidden from screen readers and inert. A click, tap or Enter opens it: it can then be used, has a close button ("Close the card"), and holds while the pointer crosses other markers. Enter moves focus into it; Escape there closes it and returns focus to the marker without closing the map. A tap on the open map closes it.
    - The card names each place at the marker, its proposed site and rank (places sharing one site name it once, a line each). Below that are the chapters that name a place: "Bethel · 69 verses in 35 chapters", then the chapter before this verse's (‹), this verse ("Genesis 28:19 · this verse") and the chapter after (›). Each is a link to the chapter's first verse that names the place, with its count when more than one verse there does.
    - "All 35 chapters naming Bethel" opens every chapter, a book to a line, grouped into Law, History, Poetry, Prophets, Gospels and Acts, and Letters and Revelation, with this chapter marked. Places sharing a marker (Jerusalem and Zion) get a switch for whose chapters to show.
    - "Nearby" names the three nearest well-known places within 15 km, in whole kilometres. It leaves out the marker's own places and anything at their sites (Luz at Bethel).
    - The card goes beside the marker, or above or below it where the box is too narrow (a phone), placed by its drawn height, so it never covers it. It stays below the controls, and a tall card scrolls. Where its full view is taller than its side of the marker allows (a short phone map), it opens compact, so the chapters before and after show without a scroll: the place chips on one row that scrolls sideways to the chosen place (or one line for a single place), with the chosen place's site, then ‹ the chapter before and the chapter after › on one row (where two long references would not fit, they are shown short: without the book in this verse's book, "‹ 5:8 · 8:4 ›" at Song of Solomon 6:4, and by the book's short name in another, "‹ 1 Kgs 15:13 · 2 Chr 15:16 ›" at 2 Kings 23:12; a carried setting's "named in" goes under the row, in tab order) and the full list's link; a swipe across the chips scrolls them and does not turn the page (`data-scrolls-sideways`); the chapter links are the same elements in both views, so keyboard focus survives the switch; the ranks, the count, this verse's row and Nearby are left out. It is hidden on a phone with Ask open and returns as Ask closes.
    - The opened card is remembered by its site while turning pages, so ‹ and › walk a place's story with the card still open. A page whose setting lacks that site closes it.
    - The places data reaches the browser only with the map's lazily loaded view.
  - **Zoom** has three named steps, *Here · The land · The Bible world*, top left under the site's name and account button (clear of them), with − and + beside them. On a computer they share a row with the close button; on a phone the close button sits level with the site's name instead, so the steps and Then · Today keep one row below it. A narrow map (under 720 px: a phone, or a small window beside the words page) shows Here · Land · World without − and +. The controls are hidden on a phone with Ask open. A step that would show the box nothing new is disabled.
    - The land is the opening frame above.
    - Here closes in to at least half a degree.
    - The Bible world is Greece and Libya to Persia, joined with the land frame.
    - A step that would show nothing new is disabled (Here for a setting of only regions).
    - The step lasts for the visit while turning pages.
    - On a computer, a locator in the map's bottom-right corner, above the note, shows where the view sits in the Bible world, except at that step, on a map under 600 px wide, or where it would cover a place the verse names. It does not change how the map frames the setting.
  - **Then · Today** sits beside Zoom (wrapping under it where the row is too narrow) and switches the map between the Bible world and today's ([today-map.json](../../src/data/today-map.json), from Natural Earth, public domain). Today darkens the land and sea, draws today's borders (Natural Earth's de facto lines; disputed and indefinite lines dashed), names up to 12 countries and up to 14 cities (8 on a narrow map) clear of the verse's markers and names, with no city dot inside a ring or a pin's tap area (Jerusalem shows at Here for Bethel, not at The land), and leaves out the faint ancient places. The verse's markers and card work as on Then. Today's names are drawn for the eye only, so the map is described to a screen reader instead ("Today's map names Israel, Jordan, and the cities Tel Aviv, Damascus and Haifa."). The choice lasts for the visit while turning pages, like the step, and is hidden on a phone with Ask open.
  - **A short map** (a phone's strip beside Ask, or a short window) keeps at least 56 px for the setting above the note by framing it up beside the controls. The steps and Then · Today are put away there (invisible and inert), and faint and Today names, the card and the locator stay below the controls. The locator also hides wherever it cannot fit between the controls and the note.
  - The note is at most three tenths of the map's height, or less where the controls and 56 px for the setting need the room, and scrolls beyond that (a verse naming many places, beside the picture), so it never takes the controls' room.
  - The occasional feedback prompt is held back while the map shows, since it sits where the controls go.
  - **What sits on the map.** A note under the map names the setting, says when it was carried ("Set at Marah, named in verse 23.") and when a site is debated. It credits "Places: OpenBible.info, CC BY 4.0 · Map: Natural Earth" on both Then and Today, so switching never changes its height; a phone's strip beside Ask keeps only the OpenBible credit. The map also has a close button ("Close the map"; it carried an Experimental tag beside it until 25 November 2026) and, for a painted verse, the picture small in its corner ("Back to the picture of …").
  - **While it shows**, the link reads "Back to the picture".
  - **Keyboard.** Opened from the keyboard, the map takes focus, so Tab walks its markers next. The link, the close button, the small picture and Escape (when no layer is open and the reader is not typing in a field) bring the picture back.
  - **It stays on while reading.** Whether the map is on lasts for the tab's visit (`useMapMode`, not a layer), so turning pages keeps it, and a reload starts with the picture. A verse without a setting shows its picture and no link; the whole-picture view shows the picture.
  - **It loads lazily.** The map and its outline load only when a reader first opens it.
  - **Analytics.** The link reports `map_link_shown` once per spread when it offers the map and is in view (on a phone a long verse can put it below the dock), and opening reports `map_opened`. Exploring the open map reports `map_explored` (zoom steps, Then · Today, card chapters); its kinds are one `MapExploration` type in [analytics.ts](../../src/lib/analytics.ts), shared with the map.
  - **Data.** `npm run places:build` regenerates both data files from OpenBible.info and Natural Earth, and `npm run places:today` regenerates today's map ([scripts/places](../../scripts/places/README.md)).
- **Layers** ([layers.tsx](../../src/components/spread/layers.tsx)): Look, Contents, the corner menu, the picture menu, Ask and the whole-picture view open over the spread, one at a time, never as browser history. Escape closes the open one and focus returns to the control that opened it. A panel layer is a modal dialog: focus starts on its `data-autofocus` control or its first shown control, and Tab stays inside it, skipping controls not shown at this size (a phone-only close button on a computer) and counting a radio group as one stop, its checked radio, as the browser does ([focus-trap.ts](../../src/lib/focus-trap.ts)). The credits dialog, which opens over any layer, keeps its own trap the same way. Every layer that opens as a phone sheet has a visible close control as well (Done, a close button, or Back), so nothing is reachable only by a gesture.
- **Contents** ([contents.tsx](../../src/components/spread/contents.tsx)) is the one way to move around the Bible. It shows the current chapter as a wall: one numbered tile per verse with its picture from a thumbnail ([picture-source.ts](../../src/lib/picture-source.ts)), a count when the verse has more than one picture (in any style, "9+" past nine), a dashed unpainted tile otherwise, the play mark in the top-left corner of a tile whose own picture has a finished video (its name ends ", has a video"; never on an unpainted tile), and the current verse ringed and focused. The book's name at the top opens the book list with a type-to-filter field (Enter takes the first match); choosing a book shows its first chapter. The chapter row under it switches the wall without leaving Contents; it scrolls only as far as it must to show the current chapter clear of its edge fades, so a chapter that already fits leaves it at chapter 1. Choosing a tile opens that verse and closes Contents. The arrow keys never turn the page while it is open. Opening it reports `contents_opened`; a tile reports `verse_navigation` with source `contents` and direction `jump`.
- **Painting the chapter** ([chapter-paint.tsx](../../src/components/spread/chapter-paint.tsx)): when the chapter on the wall has verses with no picture in the chosen art style (a tile showing another style's picture still counts), one button above the wall reads "Paint the N unpainted verses · about X credits", or "Paint N verses in <Style> · about X credits" when some of those tiles show another style's picture, and starts the existing bulk job for exactly those verses; see [Bulk generation](BULK-GENERATION.md).
- **The Contents read** (`getChapterContents` in [verseImages.ts](../../convex/verseImages.ts)) is one query per chapter given the verse ids and an ordered list of art styles. Per verse it reads the newest few rows to count (at most 10), the newest picture in each style from the `by_verse_artStyle` index until one is found, and at most one finished video of that tile picture from `videoJobs.by_source_status` (`hasVideo`), so its cost does not grow with the number of saved pictures or videos. The style list is the picture rule's order, so a tile shows the picture the spread would.
- **Look** ([look.tsx](../../src/components/spread/look.tsx)), the `look` layer, holds how the book appears. "Aa" in the dock opens it (above the dock on a computer, a sheet from the bottom with **Done** on a phone), and so does the translation name on the words page ("World English Bible, Look"). It has three choices, each saved at once: **Art style**, a radio group of the styles the reader may see, each shown by a small picture (`getStyleExamples`: the verse's own newest picture in that style, else Genesis 1:1's, else a small fixed example shipped in [public/style-examples](../../public/style-examples/README.md) for each library style, else a plain tile with the style's initial); choosing one swaps the spread's picture in place. **Translation**, a native select grouped English and Other, laid transparent over a pill that shows the chosen name and a chevron (the pill sizes to the name and matches the text size control's height), with its notice under it ("Scripture quotations are from the World English Bible (WEB), 2000."); choosing one writes the cookie and refreshes the route, so the server renders the verse and the words page's name in it. **Text size**, three steps (Small, Medium, Large) that change only the words page's verse. Focus starts on the chosen style; Escape closes Look and returns focus to the control that opened it. Opening it reports `look_opened` with source `dock` or `translation_name`; each change reports `preference_changed` with source `look` ([Preferences](PREFERENCES.md)).
- **The account chip** (in [corner-menu.tsx](../../src/components/spread/corner-menu.tsx)): beside the Visibible name, inside the same button, a small glass chip shows a person and the available balance ("1,228"; holds are already deducted), so a reader sees their credits on every spread without opening anything. An admin sees a shield and no number; while the balance loads or cannot be read the chip shows the person alone, never a zero. The button keeps its name, "Visibible menu", and the chip's words are its description ("1,228 credits, 12 held", "Admin, not charged", "Loading balance", "Balance unavailable"). It fades with the name and hides in the whole-picture view, where the name stays. It is where a profile would live if Visibible gets accounts.
- **The corner menu** ([corner-menu.tsx](../../src/components/spread/corner-menu.tsx)): the Visibible name (with its account chip) in the top-left corner opens one menu, the `corner` layer. It opens on the account: "Guest · Kept in this browser" ("Admin" for an admin, "Account" until the session is read), the credit balance (with held credits) and **Get credits**, or "Admin · not charged" for an admin, and **Recover credits** (`/recover`); then **Send feedback**; **About**; **API documentation**; the social links ([site-links.tsx](../../src/components/site-links.tsx)); and, last, **Advanced**, collapsed until opened ([Advanced](ADVANCED.md)). The menu scrolls when Advanced makes it taller than the window. A page opened with `?admin` also shows **Admin sign-in** under Recover credits, which opens the credits dialog at its Admin Access section; without the parameter a visitor sees nothing about admin. **Send feedback** replaces the menu with the feedback form, the `feedback` layer ([Feedback](FEEDBACK.md)). Both are [layers](../../src/components/spread/layers.tsx): one open at a time, Escape, a click outside or, on a phone, the menu's close button ("Close menu", beside the account) closes them, and focus returns to the name.
- **The credits dialog** opens only when the reader asks for it (Get credits or Admin sign-in here, and later paint or ask). It follows the reader's appearance ([Theme](THEME.md)).
- **Reduced motion** makes every slide, fade and spinner instant (one rule in globals.css).
- **Over the picture** a soft shade runs along the top so the white corner name and marks stay legible on a bright sky; Contents tiles shade their number the same way.
- **The address** is the verse's alone. No dialog opens by itself. Any `?view=` or `?image=` parameter is ignored: a chosen picture version is never part of the address, on a prose moment too.
- **The server-rendered page** keeps the verse text, title and description. A passage the translation lacks, or an outage, renders [PassageUnavailable](../../src/components/passage-unavailable.tsx) under the plain site header.

The unavailable-passage page has its own [translation picker](../../src/components/translation-selector.tsx), so a reader can stay at the passage in another translation.

## Prose works

`/read` is the library: the Bible, and each prose work in the [catalog](../../src/lib/works/catalog.ts) the viewer may open. `/read/{work}` lists the work's chapters or stories, and `/read/{work}/{moment}` shows one passage. A passage is one moment of the work's active analysis, the same run of paragraphs the image engine draws, and its address is the moment's first unit (`/read/alice-in-wonderland/the-pool-of-tears-1`). [prose-book.ts](../../src/lib/works/prose-book.ts) cuts the imported text (`works/{id}/work.json`) by the active generation's moments, cached per generation for up to a minute on each server instance, so an owner correction to a moment reaches the page within a minute. A work with no active analysis is not found.

- **Who can open a work.** A work is the owner's preview until it is listed in `PUBLIC_PROSE_WORK_IDS` ([proseWorks.ts](../../convex/lib/proseWorks.ts)); the catalog reads that list. The owner here is an admin session ([viewer.ts](../../src/lib/works/viewer.ts)); the configured owner-check session is not enough.
  - For anyone else, an unopened work answers exactly like a work that does not exist: its pages are not found, the library leaves it out, image requests get the same 404, and `/api/image-models?work=` gives the Bible's answer.
  - Convex's image reads by verse id (`getImageHistory`, `getChapterContents`; the internal `getChapterImageStatus` and `getChaptersWithImages` apply the same rule) leave out an unopened work's saved images unless the caller's guest token belongs to an admin session.
  - visibible.com serves only the Bible to readers. Holmes and Alice stay the owner's preview: they verify that the engine and reader work for any work. Other books launch on a separate site built from this codebase. Nothing in public navigation links to `/read`.
- **The passage page** (`/read/{work}/{moment}`, #451) is the same [spread](../../src/components/spread/spread.tsx) as a verse, inside a [work scope](../../src/context/work-scope-context.tsx), the spread's work-scope switch. Everything above holds for it, with these differences:
  - The words page names the work, heads the passage with its division's name ("Down the Rabbit-Hole") and shows its paragraphs, Gutenberg's `_underscores_` as italics ([prose-text.tsx](../../src/components/spread/prose-text.tsx)), in a smaller serif than a verse at each of Look's text sizes; they scroll inside the page. The foot line has the position in the division ("3 / 12", read as "Passage 3 of 12") and no translation.
  - Copy says "passage" where a verse page says "verse": "Previous passage" and "Next passage", "Paint this passage", "Paint passages automatically as I read", the marks ("Passages of Down the Rabbit-Hole", "Passage 2, painted") and Contents tiles (`useMomentWord`).
  - Previous and next (dock, arrow keys, swipe) move between the work's passages in reading order, across divisions. The marks show the division's passages. The whole-picture view's caption is the passage's first paragraph, cut to four lines.
  - Pictures are kept under the moment's id and painted with the work and unit; the picture rule, the picture menu and Look offer the work's brief styles (a saved style the work lacks falls back to its first style without being overwritten). The unpainted glow is the nearest earlier painted passage of the division: the page names the division's earlier moments, nearest first, to `getNearestEarlierPicture` (`earlier`, at most 200).
  - **Look** has art style and text size, no translation. **Contents** shows the work's title, its divisions by number (each button named "Chapter 2, The Pool of Tears"; a work's top level names them, "Story" for Holmes), the chosen division's name, and its passages as tiles by the same picture rule. Choosing a tile opens it. There is no chapter painting job.
  - **Ask** is not offered: no foot, no phone dock button. The phone dock holds Contents and Aa. Advanced leaves out the answering model and passage details.
  - The verse analytics (`verse_view`) are the Bible's only; page turns report `verse_navigation` with the work's title as the book.
- **The library and a work's page** (`/read`, `/read/{work}`) are plain pages under the site header: the library's cards, and the work's title, author, Start reading and its chapters or stories.
- **Not on prose pages:** Ask, video, chapter painting and the public image API.

### Serving a work on another site

The generic check closed on 10 November 2026: in production, Holmes and Alice imported, prepared, read and generated through the same engine, image route and reader as the Bible, and stayed hidden from everyone but an admin session. A site built from this codebase to serve other books needs these changes. None of them belongs on visibible.com.

- **Add the work to the build.** Commit its source snapshot under `works/sources/` (listed in `manifest.json`), add an importer to [import.mts](../../scripts/works/import.mts) and commit its approved `works/{id}/brief.json`. `works/*/work.json` is ignored by git and rebuilt by `npm run build`, which runs the importer first, so a work without an importer has no text in a clean deployment. Its unit ids must not collide with another work's, the Bible's included: saved images are keyed by unit id alone.
- **Register and open it.** Add the work and its brief to `PROSE_WORKS` in [catalog.ts](../../src/lib/works/catalog.ts); the reader and image route reject any id that is not there. Then add its id to `PUBLIC_PROSE_WORK_IDS` ([proseWorks.ts](../../convex/lib/proseWorks.ts)). That list lives in Convex, so the change needs a Convex deploy as well as a site deploy; the catalog, image route and Convex image reads all follow it.
- **Link it.** Add a `/read` link to public navigation and sitemap entries for `/read` and each open work, and set the sitemap's `BASE_URL` to the new site's origin. Commit `582872f` (#408, later reverted here by #409) added the link and entries, in [footer.tsx](../../src/components/footer.tsx) and [sitemap.ts](../../src/app/sitemap.ts).
- **Prepare it in that site's Convex.** Each work needs an active analysis, canon and reference sheets there ([work input](WORK-INPUT.md), [work preparation](WORK-PREPARATION.md)). A work with no active analysis is not found.
- **Styles.** A work offers the styles its brief lists that are also in `PUBLIC_GENERIC_STYLE_IDS` ([admission.ts](../../src/lib/engine/admission.ts)).
- **Nostr.** The scheduler skips every image whose work is not the Bible, because a post links to a verse page ([NOSTR.md](NOSTR.md)). A site that posts its images changes that check and the post's link.
- **Known limits.** Alice's court and croquet scenes reached 0–1 of 9 at the launch bar from image-model habits ([record](../releases/2026-11-10-alice-court-fix/README.md)). Holmes reaches 19–24 of 30.
