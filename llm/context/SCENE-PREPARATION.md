# Section looks and scene plans

[Lazy preparation](../../src/lib/engine/preparation.ts) makes the two pieces of text the generic engine needs before it can draw a moment, the first time they are needed, and stores them in the work's active analysis generation ([Analysis storage](ANALYSIS-STORAGE.md)). The legacy route's scene planner and its `scenePlanCache` are untouched and separate.

- **Section look**: one per story section, shared by every image of that section in every art style and reader translation. It should hold only the situational place, time of day, weather, light and clothing, leaving fixed appearance to canon. Its inputs are the section version, the brief version and the canon versions of the section's cast, plus the look-prompt version when the cast includes a no-form entry.
- **Scene plan**: one per moment, shared by every art style and reader translation. Claude Haiku 5.5 sees the moment's reference text, its neighbours inside the same section (a section boundary gives none), the brief and era, the section look and the section's cast, and returns a required source-grounding audit, which cast are present, a required `missingCast` list, and subject, action, setting, secondary elements, mood, time and composition. Code pairs each present entry with the exact stage the section assigns it. A missing entry or missing/blank assigned stage stops preparation before model calls; it never substitutes the first stage. Assembly rereads the section after planning and requires its version and selected stage assignments to match, then checks each selected entry version and exact stage, refusing a concurrent correction before reference loading. Inputs: the moment, section, look, brief and the cast's canon versions, the selected-cast prompt version, and the additional presence-prompt version when the cast includes a no-form entry. The direct section dependency refreshes a reassigned stage even when the regenerated look has identical content. Nothing about a style or a translation enters the plan or its identity, so rendering-only changes never create per-style copies.

**Shared context does not select cast.** The planner must establish presence in the scene being illustrated. A shared wardrobe, a narrator's viewpoint or a neighboring mention alone does not establish presence. Neighbors still resolve identity and continuity within the current scene; they do not import cast from a separate adjacent event. Every cast entry described in a plan field must be selected in `present`, and shared clothing and props apply only to selected entries. Since moment-cast v6, people, crowds, roles and animals with no cast entry may be described by role and situation as incidental figures without fixed appearance; they are never given a recurring character's name. For a narrated story, parable, dream, vision or recollection, instructions select the actors and setting of the action being illustrated. Its framing speakers/listeners do not become actors inside it, and reporting “I saw” supplies a viewpoint rather than a visible body. A shared look from the framing scene does not establish the inner scene’s cast or place. These are model instructions. The source-grounding checks below require a current-moment anchor for characters and groups while allowing neighbor-supported identity and continuity, and let a selected location or object continue from a neighbor; they do not validate the meaning of that evidence.

Every plan adds an input with kind `prompt`, id `scene_plan_moment_cast` and version `2026-10-23-moment-cast-v6`, and one with id `scene_plan_review` and version `2026-10-23-plan-review-v2`. A missing or older marker refreshes that plan on next use through ordinary claims, storage and owner-funded preparation, reusing a current look. It applies to formed and no-form cast alike. The look requests, schemas and versions are unchanged by this contract. Both formed and no-form plans use the new response envelope and strict selected-ID checks. There is no sweep, reread or canon edit. The additive audit schema must reach a Convex deployment before application code that stores the new field runs there (see below). The rendering packet separately names the selected characters, including groups and no-form subjects, in a protected block. Its engine rules prohibit shared looks, source narrators and neighbors from adding cast, and apply shared wardrobe only to selected characters. A look mentioning an unselected character remains stored and sent verbatim, including owner prose; code does not parse names out of free text. The instructions cannot guarantee that a model omits that character.

**One source-grounded instant.** The single planner response starts with `grounding`: a short exact `focusQuote` from the current moment, one evidence record for each selected ID in any order, a one-sentence stated `instant` and a `singleInstant` assessment. The planner may add records for section cast it considered and left out, classified as not `in_scene`; they select no one and are not quote-checked. Each entry needs a current-moment quote anchoring participation, plus an optional exact quote from the supplied previous or next unit to resolve identity, ownership or continuity. Pronouns and continued settings are allowed. A neighbor cannot provide the entire presence claim for a character or group; a selected location or object (by its `entryKind`) may be anchored by an exact neighbor quote alone. Groups are stored as characters, so they keep the current-moment anchor. Neighbor evidence is checked against the exact 400-character excerpt sent to the planner, and absent neighbors cannot be cited. Only formatting whitespace and letter case are normalized (a quoted clause often loses its sentence-initial capital); words and punctuation must match. A quote may skip words only at an explicit ellipsis (… or ...): each fragment must then be exact, start and end on word boundaries (a contraction is one word; a possessive's stem may be quoted), and appear after the previous one in the same source, so elision cannot reorder text or borrow from another unit. Unmarked joins are refused, because a guessed sentence break such as an abbreviation could splice a quote the source never contains. The moment and its same-section neighbors now precede the cast/look context in the request, and shared conditions follow the source choice instructions.

The planner chooses one representative instant from multi-action or multi-unit text. After the cast evidence it states that instant once in `grounding.instant`: where each selected formed character is and their single pose or movement, and the placement of selected places, objects and no-form effects. It writes the seven scene fields from that sentence. Formed plans' field descriptions tie the subject, action and composition to it. The stated instant is model guidance and audit data; code only requires it to be nonblank and cannot compare it with the scene prose. It classifies each selected entry as `in_scene`, `framing`, `mentioned`, `separate_event` or `uncertain`. Code refuses a selected entry not reported as `in_scene`, a false `singleInstant`, a blank stated instant, a selected entry without evidence, repeated or unknown evidence IDs, an unselected entry claimed `in_scene`, a focus outside the moment, an ungrounded current participation quote or invalid neighbor support. Each refused reply records one `unavailable` preparation call. Code never filters the cast or edits a reply. Missing or malformed response fields still use the existing one schema/error retry. `missingCast` remains the immediate source-gap path and is never re-planned.

Successful grounding is retained beside the plan as optional audit data in analysis storage, included in its content version, and excluded from the scene fields and image prompt. Historical plans without it, and stored v3 audits without a stated instant, remain readable. The v4 marker lazily invalidates older plans, including stored v3 plans, without remaking a current look; saved images and their versions stay intact. Deploy the additive Convex schema before deploying a server that writes this audit field. Production received the v3 field on 23 October 2026 ([backend deployment](../releases/2026-10-23-grounded-plan-check/deployment.json)); production received the optional `instant` later the same day ([v4 deployment](../releases/2026-10-23-instant-plan-check/deployment.json)). One plan response contains both evidence and scene prose; its output ceiling is 2,400 tokens (expected completion allowance 900); looks retain 900/350.

**Plan review and one re-plan.** The owner approved this on 23 October 2026 after [repeated blind-scored samples](../releases/2026-10-23-grounding-rate-check/README.md) showed prompt rules alone were not converging. Each plan that passes the evidence checks is reviewed before storage by a second Claude Haiku 5.5 call with reasoning effort `low` and a 4,000-token output ceiling ([`plan-review.ts`](../../src/lib/engine/plan-review.ts)). The setting travels on each review request, so every adapter (request path, owner scripts, evaluations) uses the benchmarked configuration while planning keeps reasoning off. The review sees only what reaches rendering (selected IDs and the seven scene fields), the moment and its neighbor excerpts, the section cast with `hasForm`, and the depiction rules. It reports problems of four kinds:

- `unselected_figure`: a person, group or animal with a cast entry is described but not selected.
- `outside_instant`: a framing narrator or listener, or a figure or selected place from another step, appears.
- `not_one_instant`: the fields give one character incompatible poses or successive steps.
- `embodied_formless`: a no-form subject has a body or is seated.

Code accepts a report only when every problem quotes the plan exactly (whitespace and case aside) or names a selected ID, and names a cast ID of the right kind. A report failing that twice makes the plan unavailable rather than passing it unchecked.

A plan rejected by the evidence checks or the review gets exactly one re-plan. The same request is extended with the rejected problems as feedback. The replacement must pass both checks; a second rejection stops preparation as a retryable `unavailable` before references load, a request record is created or credits are reserved. A review call alone takes about 4.7 s median (p90 6.1 s) and costs about $0.0005; a plan and its review took 8.3 s median in the held-out run. A re-plan adds a plan and a review. Claims last 120 s and waiters poll 90 s, enough for the worst case of two plan replies and two reviews with their malformed-reply retries. Stored plans retain `review: { rounds, evidence?, problems? }` as audit data excluded from the image prompt. The extra call likely pushes the measured cold full-request p90 (17.75 s) past its 20 s target; that gate needs a fresh measurement and an owner decision. A cache miss still costs owner preparation spend.

The review was chosen on a [pre-declared benchmark](../releases/2026-10-23-plan-review-check/README.md) of the 40 blind-scored replies. Reasoning off flagged every clean plan. Reasoning low cut false alarms to 7/25 but flagged next-verse scenery that a re-plan cannot remove. Prompt v2, limited to figures and selected places, caught 13/15 defective plans with 3/25 false alarms. It is reliable for unselected figures and seated no-form subjects, and weak for single-instant conflicts. It was tuned on that benchmark. The [held-out run](../releases/2026-10-23-reviewed-plan-check/README.md) of the whole pipeline on fresh samples stored only 1 flawed plan in 20 (v3 7, v4 3) and refused no clean plan; its 9 refusals came 6 when the review rejected the re-plan and 3 when the re-plan failed the evidence checks. It still produced only 10 acceptable stored plans: Daniel 7:9 was refused all five times and Luke 10:34 three times. Most re-plans traded one problem for another. Text preparation took 11.9 s median and 18.5 s p90 with the look already stored. A [random sample of 100 ordinary verses](../releases/2026-10-23-random-verse-check/README.md) stored acceptable plans for 53 (43–62%), flawed plans for 6, and refused 41. Of those refusals, 23 were missing-cast reports (mostly generic roles), 12 evidence checks (10 with acceptable scenes) and 6 reviews (5 acceptable). The same planner without review reached 48 acceptable and 5 flawed. Preparation including the look took 12.9 s median and 19.1 s p90.

The evidence checks establish quote membership and reject the model's own reported conflicts, **not semantic correctness**; the review is a separate model judgment, not a guarantee. A real quote can be irrelevant, “I watched” can be falsely classified `in_scene`, and successive actions can be falsely assessed simultaneous. [Grounding regressions](../../src/lib/__tests__/scene-grounding.test.ts) retain those limits explicitly alongside the exact Luke 10:34 and Daniel 7:9 v2 first replies, the live v3 Luke 10:33 pose contradiction (which still passes every check), the refused Daniel 7:10 unmarked join (still refused; accepted once marked with an ellipsis), abbreviation, partial-word, contraction, ordered, reordered and invented fragment cases, malformed-contract refusal, reported framing/sequence failures, same-section excerpt boundaries, non-Bible embedded/multi-unit prose and reuse across styles. Older canon/look tests add synthetic model assertions while preserving their scene prose; those assertions do not rehabilitate historical failures. The actual request-path test proves a reported sequence stops before image billing. The [first live v3 replies](../releases/2026-10-23-grounded-plan-check/README.md) removed the retained Luke 10:33 and Daniel 7:9 presence failures, but none passed. Luke 10:33 still gave the Samaritan successive poses with `singleInstant` true. Daniel 7:9 described its no-form presence as seated in an unoccupied seat. Daniel 7:10 was refused because its neighbor quote joined two non-adjacent clauses. V4 answers the pose and elision gaps with guidance and an explicit-ellipsis rule. Its [first live replies](../releases/2026-10-23-instant-plan-check/README.md) gave Luke 10:33 one consistent pose but described an unselected donkey, and Daniel 7:9 restored its narrator and next-verse attendants. A [blind-scored repeated-sample comparison](../releases/2026-10-23-grounding-rate-check/README.md) of v3 and v4 (five replies per moment each) found neither reliable: 10/20 and 8/20 acceptable stored plans. V4 stored fewer flawed plans but refused more clean ones, mostly because the model added evidence records for figures it had correctly left out. Seated-presence and denial wording for no-form subjects remain model-obedience gaps. No improved image, repeatability or human acceptance is established; inspect first ordinary replies before funding images.

**Presence without a body.** For sections whose cast includes `hasForm: false`, both look and plan requests receive each cast member's ID, name and `hasForm` flag and constraints from the exact section-assigned no-form canon stage; a missing stage is an error rather than a fallback. Newly staged structured canon retains its `showing` choices. When those choices validate and compile to the exact current stage text, both requests receive only the unique effect names, such as `radiance, fire, cloud, light`; fixed colors and brightness stay in the complete canon supplied to rendering. Historical prose stages, missing/invalid choices, or a prose edit that no longer matches the choices keep the authoritative stage text as context. Code never infers effect choices from prose or silently updates an owner's text. Formed entries' canon text is not added to either request.

Instructions require the permitted effects and their placement rather than bodily poses, silhouettes, clothing or negated substitutes such as a faceless figure. Bodily source metaphors about a no-form subject do not grant a physical form: a seated metaphor becomes an unoccupied seat with the permitted surrounding effects. Formed characters retain their own poses, clothing and actions. Look instructions restrict clothing to formed characters' situational clothing and shared props, and effects to the shared setting or illumination without fixed canon qualifiers. They do not force every section cast member into the shared setting. The source quotation is unchanged. Historical looks and model replies may still contain appearance language: these instructions and the canon projection do not enforce depiction semantics.

**Canon and placement.** The complete canon is supplied separately to rendering. No-form planning instructions therefore ask for only the permitted effect needed to describe this moment's placement or movement, leaving its fixed color, brightness and texture in canon. A generic example pairs canon `The presence: brilliant gold radiance` with plan `radiance spreading above the arch; the witness approaches`. The subject, action and secondary-element field descriptions repeat this boundary. The planner should not enumerate every canon effect or copy its qualifiers into another field. Situational illumination and formed characters' clothing remain valid planning details; code does not ban color or lighting words, strip adjectives, or modify the assigned canon, owner look or source text.

The planner must still include a no-form entry in `present` when describing its presence or action and select only the cast present in this moment; code does not append the whole section cast. Missing legacy flags mean `true`, consistently with rendering and reference selection. Selected no-form entries supply their assigned canon stage and the rendering packet's protected depiction constraints, but never a sheet. Absent entries supply neither canon nor references to the rendering packet, even though their canon was available as section context to the planner.

Every plan request uses a strict response schema that restricts `present` to the known section cast IDs and locally rejects duplicate IDs. An empty section cast permits only an empty selection; the provider schema uses string items in that case rather than an empty enum, and local validation rejects every supplied ID. For no-form requests, local validation also enforces the existing 220-character limit on each of the seven scene fields. [Anthropic does not support `maxLength` in raw output schemas](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs#json-schema-limitations), so each field describes its limit while a Zod refinement checks it locally without serializing that unsupported keyword. An empty `present` array is allowed; the schema does not force any cast into a moment. Descriptions on the response fields guide the model toward visible effects, placement and formed characters' actions, consistent with the same system instructions and supplied canon. An unknown cast ID or overlong field is an invalid response, handled by the existing single schema/error retry. After two invalid attempts, preparation fails before reference loading or any image call; code does not silently filter IDs or truncate fields on these requests.

**Missing required canon.** `missingCast` contains nonblank names required by the moment but absent from the available section cast. Since moment-cast v6 (owner decision of 23 October 2026), it is reserved for a named character or group the story follows beyond the moment, whose look must stay consistent across images. Roles, crowds, animals, people named only in passing and available cast members are never reported. A cast member whose presence is uncertain leads to a different instant, not a report. A moment without an event (a list, law, saying or greeting) still gets a scene illustrating its content or setting. In the [random-verse sample](../releases/2026-10-23-random-verse-check/README.md), 23 of 100 verses had been refused this way, mostly for generic roles. The model is instructed to return empty scene fields and selection instead of substituting framing figures. A nonempty report stops immediately with retryable “Preparing scene: plan unavailable: required canon unavailable: …”, records that one provider call as `unavailable` owner spend, leaves any earlier plan untouched, and loads no references or image provider. It is not a malformed response to retry until the gap disappears. The field is required even when empty and is not persisted in successful scene plans or rendering prompts. A schema-invalid reply still receives the existing bounded schema/error retry.

The reading-pass instructions now include actors and settings inside embedded stories and visions, distinguish their roles from the framing participants, and require `on_stage`/`mentioned_only` to be disjoint. Range validation rejects overlap between those lists with ordinary bounded correction feedback. This structural check does not discover omitted actors. Existing analysis is unchanged; source corrections still require grounded identities, canon and section assignments through owner tools, followed by fresh ordinary text checks before images. No rerun of the reading pass or automatic canon creation is performed by this change.

Affected looks use the same five required string fields, with field descriptions and a local 200-character limit. As with plans, the serialized schema omits unsupported `maxLength`; an oversized field uses the existing single schema/error retry, without truncation. These looks add an input with kind `prompt`, id `section_look_formless_presence` and version `2026-10-22-formless-look-v1`. A missing or older marker refreshes the look lazily through normal claims and storage, retaining the previous version in history. Plans depend on the look's content hash: changed look text invalidates the dependent plan; identical text updates the look's input provenance without funding another already-current plan. The marker is not a semantic acceptance stamp.

No-form plans additionally retain their input with kind `prompt`, id `scene_plan_formless_presence` and version `2026-10-22-formless-effects-v5`. A missing or older no-form marker, including v1–v4, refreshes that plan on next use, reusing a current look. Adding `showing` to a staged entry changes its content hash, so its look and dependent plans invalidate through ordinary entry inputs. This code does not backfill live canon, restage a work or immediately regenerate stored records. Sections whose cast all have form keep their previous look request/cache and scene-field schema; their plan response also rejects unknown/duplicate IDs and requires `missingCast` and `grounding`. No plan silently filters selected IDs. Once deployed, affected cache misses incur ordinary owner-funded preparation calls; this change adds no sweep or deployment.

`prepareMoment` returns the look, the plan, the present entries with their stages and canon versions, and the number of calls it made. A record whose stored inputs no longer match the current versions is remade; an owner version is never overwritten, since a remake writes a new generated version only where the owner's inputs changed, and `clearRecord` on a plan simply forces a future replan. Saved images are never touched.

**Concurrency.** Two requests missing the same record take a claim in Convex (`claimPreparation`); the holder funds the call and releases the claim, the others poll for the record for up to 45 s and otherwise return a retryable error. Within one server instance the same promise is shared. `getPreparationStatus` reports `missing`, `preparing` or `ready` for a moment's look and plan so the reader can see "Preparing scene".

**Failure.** A model error or invalid JSON is retried once; then `PreparationUnavailableError` (retryable) is raised before any image call. There is no unplanned prompt and no legacy fallback. Every attempt, successful or not, is written to `preparationLedger` as owner spend (`getPreparationSpend`); no guest credit is reserved or charged for preparation.

**In the request path.** The generic engine ([generation.ts](../../src/lib/engine/generation.ts)) calls `prepareMoment` after resolving the verse's moment from the deployed work and before building the rendering packet; its "Preparing scene" 503 carries `getPreparationStatus` for that moment so the reader can be told whether the look or plan is missing or being made. See [Image generation](IMAGE-GENERATION.md#generic-generation-path).
